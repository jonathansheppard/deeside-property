About these figures

Every sale of a home in England and Wales is registered with HM Land Registry. It publishes the price, date and address of each sale once a month.

The figures run two to three months behind. A sale only appears once the buyer's solicitor has registered it, so the most recent weeks always look quieter than they really were. They fill in over the following months.

The typical prices use ordinary sales only. Land Registry marks some sales as additional: repossessions, buy-to-let purchases with a mortgage, sales to companies and transfers that are not on the open market. These are still listed, labelled "Not a standard sale", but are left out of the typical prices because they can pull the figures up or down.

The typical price is the median, the sale in the middle when every sale is lined up from cheapest to dearest. It is less thrown by a single mansion or a cheap flat than an average. Sales not completed for value, such as gifts and right to buy, are not in the Land Registry data at all.

Leasehold means the buyer owns the home for a fixed number of years but not the land it stands on, and usually pays ground rent and other charges to a freeholder. It is normal for flats. For houses it is less common and has been the subject of a national scandal over rising ground rents, so it is shown separately.

Places are the town or village Land Registry gives for each address. Where it gives none, the postal town is used.
